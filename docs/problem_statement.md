# Problem Statement: Retail Investor Fraud in India

## 1. Context & The Democratization of Demat Accounts
Over the past five years, India has witnessed unprecedented retail participation in equity markets:
- Active demat accounts surged from ~3.5 crore in 2019 to over 15 crore by 2024.
- More than 70% of incremental retail investors originate from **Tier-2, Tier-3, and rural areas** across states like Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, and Bihar.
- Many new investors have limited prior exposure to financial concepts such as risk-reward trade-offs, derivatives, market volatility, or the regulatory mandate of SEBI.

## 2. The Attack Vectors & Scammer Tactics

### A. The "Guaranteed Return" & Upper Circuit Illusion
Unregulated operators operate large networks of Telegram channels and WhatsApp groups (frequently labeled "VIP Premium Club", "Sure Shot Jackpot", "100% Upper Circuit Call").
- **Tactic:** Fake promises of doubling money in 7 to 30 days, or guaranteed daily gains of ₹15,000–₹50,000 with "zero risk".
- **Modus Operandi:** Unscrupulous operators accumulate illiquid micro-cap shares, pump misleading tips across hundreds of broadcast groups to induce buying frenzy, and dump their holdings onto unsuspecting first-time retail investors.

### B. Identity Impersonation of Legitimate SEBI Intermediaries
- Fraudsters clone names, official logos, and registration details of genuine SEBI-registered Investment Advisers (RIAs) and Research Analysts (RAs).
- They create fabricated "SEBI Registration Certificates" decorated with gold seals, fake Ministry of Finance endorsements, or SEBI Chairman signatures.
- Communication is routed entirely through anonymous handles on Telegram or WhatsApp, while payments are collected via personal savings accounts or disposable UPI QR codes.

### C. Digital Demat Account Phishing & Social Engineering
- Fake KYC suspension warnings sent via SMS ("Your Demat KYC is expired, click here to avoid share freeze").
- Fraudulent customer support profiles tricking elderly users into entering their UPI PINs under the pretext of "receiving contest prizes" or "authenticating payout refunds".

## 3. The Grievance Void: Where Do Victims Go?
When retail investors realize they have been defrauded, they encounter severe structural friction:
1. **Confusion of Jurisdiction:** Investors do not know whether to report to their stock broker, the stock exchange (NSE/BSE), the depository (NSDL/CDSL), the regulator (SEBI SCORES 2.0), or local police cyber cells (Helpline 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in).
2. **Missing the "Golden Hour":** In financial cybercrime, immediate reporting within 2 to 4 hours to National Cybercrime Helpline 1930 is vital to freeze illicit transactions before money is cashed out through mule accounts.
3. **Linguistic Barriers:** Official portals often default to complex legal English, intimidating non-English speakers and elderly citizens.
4. **Lack of Preparedness:** Victims do not know what documents (contract notes, bank statements, transaction UTRs, chat logs) are required for their complaints to be actionable.

## 4. The Challenge for SANGYAN Hackathon
To create a high-empathy, accessible, multilingual digital defense system that empowers retail investors to **Detect** fraud before sending funds, **Understand** the psychological tricks being played on them, and **Act** decisively through official grievance mechanisms if aggrieved.
